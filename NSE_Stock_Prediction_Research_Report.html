1. Motivation & Primary Question

Does the best ML model for NSE stock-return prediction change depending on forecast horizon and market regime, and do apparent predictive gains survive leakage-controlled validation and realistic trading costs?

This study provides a controlled, leakage-safe empirical answer using 10 years of Indian equity data, 5 model families, 2 horizons, 3 volatility regimes, and 6 chronological test periods.

Key Practical Takeaways

  1. For NSE daily trading at 20 bps costs: the Naive baseline (buy-and-hold the signal) outperforms all ML models on risk-adjusted net return. High-frequency ML signals are cost-inefficient.

  2. For lower-frequency (5-day) strategies: Naive and LSTM both approach B&H returns. Tree models (RF, XGB) collapse — their 5-day signals fade momentum.

  3. Feature engineering is stable: the same 44 features learned in 2015–2019 remain the most important in 2024–2025. Regime-specific features are not needed.

  4. Model selection should be horizon-aware: a model chosen for 1d AUC performs poorly at 5d. Any production system must re-evaluate per-horizon.

  5. Negative result is real: NSE large-caps are efficiently priced. After leakage-safe validation, no model achieves economically meaningful alpha at realistic costs.

11. Limitations

  • Long-only strategy only (no shorting, options, or derivatives)
  • Equal weighting — no portfolio optimisation
  • 15 stocks only (survivorship bias possible — all were top-liquid throughout 2015–2025)
  • Transaction costs fixed at 20 bps RT — actual costs vary by broker, lot size, and market impact
  • LSTM was not hyperparameter-tuned; a tuned LSTM may perform differently
  • Regime detection uses only volatility — alternative regime signals (trend, correlation) not explored
